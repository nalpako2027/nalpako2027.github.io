What can TIME reveal that a snapshot cannot? And how can it lead to better decisions? 👉 In decision-making, 
a prediction is often not enough. ⚠️ 
Cross-sectional data can tell us what is happening at a given point in time, but it cannot directly tell us 
how outcomes evolve or whether observed differences reflect meaningful changes over time (Even if cross-sectional 
data are sometimes fit into causal models, causal interpretation requires more than the application of model itself).
With longitudinal data, we can examine change, temporal ordering, and patterns that unfold over time. This gives a 
richer basis for understanding relationships—not simply a snapshot of them. 📈
But there is another step: causal analysis.
Instead of asking only “What is likely to happen?”, we can ask:
“What might actually change an outcome, and by how much?”
This is where causal analysis becomes important.

Data scientists should look beyond the model itself and first consider the decision the analysis needs to support. 
When a question involves change over time or the potential impact of an action, relying solely on a cross-sectional 
predictive model may not provide enough evidence to achieve the desired outcome.

The progression from cross-sectional → longitudinal → causal is therefore not just about using more sophisticated 
methods. It is about asking progressively more informative questions—and generating evidence that can better support 
decisions and policy design. 🔍
